# What Lithuanians were said to look like — evidence and drawing cues
Research note for the GO-style Lithuanian deck, 2026-10-10. Everything below was read directly unless marked **unverified**. Period sources are quoted as data about what was *said*, with their biases noted; they are not endorsements of racial typology.

## 1. Evidence summary

### Primary / data sources (read)

- **Gintautas Česnys, "lietuvių antropologinis tipas", Visuotinė lietuvių enciklopedija (VLE, online ed.)** — the standard Lithuanian summary of the 1952–55 USSR Academy of Sciences Baltic expedition (14 districts × 100 men = 1,322 men) plus earlier work (Brennsohn 1882, Talko-Hryncewicz 1892, Žilinskas interwar conscripts). Author confirmed as Česnys via the VLE author page (id 921).
  URL: https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/lietuviu-antropologinis-tipas/ (author: https://www.vle.lt/autorius/921)
  What it says (verbatim, main type, most of Lithuania — Žemaitija, Klaipėda region, Užnemunė, western Aukštaitija): *"aukštesni negu vidutinio ūgio (vyrų ūgis 169–171 cm), plaukai pilkšvi arba kaštoniniai, rečiau – geltoni, šiek tiek banguoti, akys daugiausia šviesios (žydros), žalsvos, pilkos (rudos labai retos), veidas ir kūnas gana plaukuoti, galvos smegeninė dalis plati (brachicefališka), kakta statoka, veidas siauras (leptoprozopinis), nosis ilgoka, smarkiai išsišovusi, tiesi ar su kuprele, tarpuakis aukštas, skruostai į plotį nuolaidūs."* Assigned to the Central European type, "tarpinei Europos šatenų juostai" (the intermediate European *brown-haired* belt), northern edge.
  Regional: East Aukštaitija + Dzūkija — shorter (168–169 cm), *"plaukai tamsesni, dažniau kaštoniniai"*, eyes still light, longer head, narrow face (East Baltic + Valdai admixture). South-east (Varėna, Šalčininkai) — darker eyes and hair, slimmer face (Ilmen-Dnieper). North-west (Kretinga, Mažeikiai) — tall (>172 cm), *"labai šviesias akis ir plaukus, stambius galvos matmenis, grubokas galvos ir veido proporcijas, nuožulnią kaktą, ryškius antakių lankus ir tarpantakį, smarkiai išsišovusią, dažniausiai kumpą, nosį"* (West Baltic type). A. Kazlauskas (1940, schoolchildren): eastern Lithuanians more often darker-eyed. Stature in the 1990s (A. Adomaitis): men 178.9 cm, women 166.3 cm.
  Confidence: **high** that this is what the Lithuanian school says; it is the best single source.

- **S. Tarakanova, L. Terentyeva, N. Cheboksarov, "Некоторые вопросы этногенеза народов Прибалтики", Советская этнография 1956 no. 2** — first synthetic report of the same expedition (≈4,500 men, 900 women, 45 local groups). Defines the six types and places Lithuanians.
  URL (PDF): https://eo.iea.ras.ru/wp-content/uploads/1956/02/eoarchive_1956_2_003_tarakanova-et-al.pdf
  Verbatim type definitions: *"Восточнобалтийский — светловолосый и светлоглазый, относительно грацильный, умеренно короткоголовый со средними абсолютными размерами головы и лица и средним ростом. Западнобалтийский — тоже светловолосый (хотя и не столь резко, как предыдущий), светлоглазый, очень массивный, мезо- или суббрахикефальный, с крупными абсолютными размерами головы и лица и высоким ростом. … Ильменско-днепровский — относительно более темноволосый, умеренно светлоглазый, сравнительно грацильный, мезокефальный … Центральноевропейский — по окраске волос и радужины глаз, а также по степени массивности близкий к ильменско-днепровскому … но характеризующийся более крупными абсолютными размерами головы, сильной брахикефалией, широким и сравнительно невысоким лицом и средним ростом."*
  Placement: Central European type *"наиболее характерен для литовцев центральных и юго-западных районов Литовской ССР, в особенности для коренного населения Занеманья (Сувалкии) и территории в низовьях Немана"*; West Baltic *"в Литве … встречается гораздо реже, преимущественно на севере и северо-западе (в Жемайтии)"*; Ilmen-Dnieper as *"заметной примеси … преимущественно среди литовцев, особенно на юго-востоке (Дзукия)"*; Valdai on the eastern margin (Aukštaitija). Also notes that the East Baltic and Central European types show *"некоторой тенденцией к уменьшению степени извилистости волос, ослаблению третичного волосяного покрова, выступанию скул, уплощенности лица, более значительному развитию складки верхнего века"* (straighter hair, lighter beard, more prominent cheekbones, flatter face, fuller upper eyelid).
  Bias: the preamble is openly ideological (denounces "bourgeois" Baltic scholars); the typology is of its period. The measurements are nonetheless the same data Česnys summarises. Confidence: **high** as to what was said.

- **Carleton S. Coon, *The Races of Europe* (1939), ch. "The North", §12, pp. 365–368** — his Lithuanian section rests mainly on M. Hesch, *Letten, Litauer, Weissrussen* (WWI prisoners of war) plus Baronas, Brennsohn, Olechnowicz, Talko-Hryncewicz.
  URL (OCR full text): https://archive.org/download/racesofeurope031695mbp/racesofeurope031695mbp_djvu.txt (record: https://archive.org/details/racesofeurope031695mbp)
  Verbatim: stature "168 or 169 cm" (if grown since WWI) or "166 and 167"; *"in body build the Lithuanians frequently approach a thick-set constitutional type, while the Letts are more characteristically linear"*; cephalic index "over 82", head length 188 mm, *"a greater tendency to euryprosopy"* (broader face); *"In pigmentation the Lithuanians are less frequently blond than the Letts … over 70 per cent of Lithuanians have skin darker than von Luschan #10 … Dark brown hair (Fischer #4-5) is found in 40 per cent of Hesch's Lithuanian series … Of the remaining shades, ash-blond is the most frequent, and the darkest grade of ash-blond (Fischer #26), is the most frequent of all single numbers. Larger series … give the Lithuanians about 7 per cent of black or nearly black hair. The vast majority of Lithuanians have mixed eyes; only 10 per cent have pure light irises … pure brown eyes number but 3 per cent. On the whole … one cannot say of the Lithuanians … that they are as blond as Scandinavians, but they are still predominantly light."* Hair "almost all in the straight category" (3 % curly; curly hair "almost always blond or light brown"). *"rounder heads with broader foreheads are more frequent … The tip [of the nose] points upward in 35 per cent of cases, and is definitely snubbed in 22 per cent … a greater diversity of nose form."* Lips "medium", slightly thicker/more everted than Letts; external eyefold in only 17 %; *"The chin form is usually rather wide and rounded"*; *"more elliptical and fewer rectangular [face] shapes"*; *"much less body hair … Only 25 per cent have a medium mustache thickness … chin and cheek fall to 17 per cent and 10 per cent"*. Class contrast (Talko-Hryncewicz 1912): gentry 172.8 cm, brown hair/blue eyes; smallholders 164.8 cm, lighter hair, mixed or brown eyes. Plates: "A Lithuanian from the region of Vilna, who shows the Ladogan affiliation"; "A Lithuanian, who although brachycephalic, belongs essentially to the same Danubian type".
  Bias: racialist framework; POW sample of military-age men, largely from the Russian-ruled east; 1930s scales. Confidence: **high** as to what was said; **moderate** as to representativeness.

- **Joseph Deniker, *The Races of Man* (1900), pp. 328–329, 345–346.**
  URL (OCR): https://archive.org/download/racesofmanoutlin00denirich/racesofmanoutlin00denirich_djvu.txt
  Verbatim: Sub-northern race — *"fair, mesocephalic, of tall stature … with angular face, turned-up nose, straight hair; it is found more especially in Northern Germany, among the Letto-Lithuanians, in Finland"*; Eastern race — *"stature somewhat short (1 m. 63 or 1 m. 64 on an average), moderately rounded head (cephalic index, 82 to 83 on the living subject), straight, light yellow or flaxen hair, square-cut face, nose frequently turned up, blue or grey eyes … White Russians, the Polieshchooki of the Pinsk marshes, and certain Lithuanians"*; *"the Lithuanians exhibit elements of the Sub-northern and Eastern race."* Confidence: **high** as to text; it is an 1900 typology, thin on Lithuanian data.

- **National Geographic, "Peoples of the Soviet Union", supplement to vol. 149 no. 2, February 1976** (paintings by Sherry Wolf and Tibor G. Toth; map design John F. Dorr; compilation Donald A. Jaeger).
  Scans read: https://openaccess.ircica.org/bitstreams/49dff06f-cc3f-431e-a829-03046c297db4/download (IRCICA record https://openaccess.ircica.org/items/29f7539f-152e-45b3-bbc4-ab673eb85cca/full) and https://www.mapworld.com.au/cdn/shop/files/HM19760202-large.jpg?v=1775993087 ; catalogue: https://dijitalkoleksiyonlar.kutuphane.itu.edu.tr/cdm/singleitem/collection/itumapsAll/id/6371/rec/2
  What it actually shows for Lithuanians: **one figure**, a young woman in folk costume — dark brown hair in plaits, white embroidered blouse, dark sleeveless bodice, woven sash, amber-coloured bead strands, a low woven crown (karūna). The text panel is purely historical/cultural ("Sword-swinging Lithuanians swept from the north across Eastern Europe in the 13th and 14th centuries … Staunchly Roman Catholic, Lithuanians remain a largely agricultural people … Lithuania's shores have been known for … their precious amber"). The legend says: *"Figures in native costumes further illustrate the variety of cultures. Though traditional dress has largely yielded to contemporary attire (above) in the western republics and in larger cities, such dress still appears in isolated regions and on festive occasions."* The only "contemporary attire" figures are the Russian group (suit and tie, cosmonaut, woman in white kerchief, hard-hatted worker).
  Verdict: the map is a costume-and-culture illustration, **not** a physical description; it gives no statement about Lithuanian looks beyond one painted face. Confidence: **high** (read from scan; the legend is small print, one or two words may be misread).

- **Modern genetics: Urnikyte et al., "Patterns of genetic structure and adaptive positive selection in the Lithuanian population from high-density SNP data", *Scientific Reports* 2019** (PMC6591479). Read via Europe PMC XML: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6591479/fullTextXML
  Says: 425 individuals from the six ethnolinguistic groups show "genetic homogeneity across Lithuanian ethnolinguistic groups"; top selection signals include pigmentation genes (SLC24A5, TYRP1, PPARD). No phenotype frequencies. Confidence: **high**; marginal relevance (it supports "light-skinned, internally fairly uniform", nothing finer).

### Everyday look 1975–85 (read / viewed)

- **Kristina Sviderskaitė, *Vakarų mados transformacijos Lietuvoje 1980–1990 metais*, MA thesis, Vilnius Academy of Arts, 2013.** URL: https://gs.elaba.lt/object/elaba:2037309/2037309.pdf
  Says: Vilnius Models House worked to Moscow plans and a *single all-USSR size table* (*"lietuviai sukdavo galvą, kaip įsigyti švarką ne per trumpomis rankovėmis"* — Lithuanians struggled to get a jacket whose sleeves were not too short); Soviet fashion vocabulary was "klasika, elegancija, santūrumas, paprastumas"; colours reaching Lithuania were *"labiau sušvelnintos"* and the environment *"daug paprastesnę ir nykesnę lyginant su Vakarais"*; ordinary people compensated for deficit by individual tailoring (*"Paprasti žmonės kompensuodavo aprangos trūkumą siūdindamiesi individualiai"*) and girls were taught to sew, knit and crochet; school uniforms were compulsory; jeans were a *"religija"*, rare and prized; 1980s street-look traits for women that did reach Lithuania: widened shoulders, oversized cuts, "cheminis sušukavimas – didelė plaukų apimtis" (perm with volume), big accessories — all "gana santūriai" (fairly restrained). ## 2. Where sources disagree, and what is stereotype vs data

- **Face shape.** Coon/Hesch: broader, more euryprosopic face, wide rounded chin, broader forehead. Česnys/Baltic expedition: *narrow* (leptoprosopic) face with a long, strongly projecting nose, in the main (western/central) type; broad, "comparatively low" face only in the 1956 Central European definition. Likely sample effects (POWs from the Russian-ruled east vs 1950s republic-wide districts). **Draw both**: a range from broad-cheeked squarish to long oval.
- **Nose.** Coon: upturned tip 35 %, snub 22 %, broad bridge, "greater diversity". Česnys: longish, strongly projecting, straight or with a hump; convex/hooked in the north-west. **Draw a mix**: straight high-bridged noses most often, some humped (Žemaitija), some short upturned (east/south-east and children).
- **Beard/body hair.** Coon: "unusual glabrousness", only 25 % medium moustache. Česnys: *"veidas ir kūnas gana plaukuoti"* (face and body fairly hairy) in the main type; the 1956 article says the East Baltic/Central European types tend to *weaker* beard growth. Unresolved; for drawing, moustaches are plausible but beards should be rare and light.
- **Blondness.** The "blond Balt" is a stereotype relative to the data. Every data source puts Lithuanians in a *brown-haired / ash-blond belt*: Česnys — ashen or chestnut most common, yellow less common; Coon — 40 % dark brown, ash-blond the most common of the rest, ~7 % near-black, only 10 % pure light eyes and only 3 % pure brown, skin mostly not the palest grade; 1956 — Central European type "по окраске волос … близкий к ильменско-днепровскому" (i.e. not the light-blond East Baltic type). The 1976 NatGeo artist painted the Lithuanian woman with dark brown hair. Platinum/flaxen blondness belongs to the north-west coast strand (Kretinga–Mažeikiai) and to children.
- **Eyes.** All sources agree: light-mixed dominates — grey, blue-grey, greenish, grey-green; pure clear blue a minority; brown rare but present, more in the east/south-east.
- **Stature.** Secular growth is large: ~164–171 cm for men measured 1880s–1950s; 178.9 cm in the 1990s. Adults of 1981 (born 1920s–1960s) fall between — roughly 172–176 cm for men on interpolation (**inference, not a sourced figure**). Build: Coon "thick-set" rather than linear; 1956 West Baltic "очень массивный" in the north-west.
- **Regional gradient (real, documented)**: lighter, taller, heavier-boned, hook-nosed toward the north-west coast; somewhat darker, shorter, slimmer-faced toward the south-east (Dzūkija, Šalčininkai). Genetics (2019) finds the groups homogeneous overall, so the gradient is one of tendency, not of kind.
- **Class gradient (1912)**: taller, browner-haired gentry vs shorter, fairer smallholders — a reminder that "type" tracked diet and region as much as descent.

## 3. Drawing cues (ranges, not a type)

Pigmentation (mix across a crowd; rough proportions reconciled from Coon/Hesch and Česnys):
- Hair: ash-blond / dark ash-blond ≈ 35–40 %; light brown to chestnut ≈ 40 %; dark brown ≈ 15 %; near-black ≈ 5 %; flaxen/golden mostly on children and a few north-westerners. Straight or faintly wavy (curls rare and then light-coloured). Grey hair on the old, as anywhere.
- Eyes: grey, blue-grey, grey-green, greenish ≈ 70–80 %; clear blue ≈ 10–15 %; hazel/brown ≈ 5–10 % (more in eastern scenes).
- Skin: fair to light-ruddy, weathered on rural and outdoor figures, freckles common on fair children; not porcelain-pale.
- Beard growth: light; moustaches moderate, beards rare and thin.

Head and face:
- Head broad and round-ish (brachycephalic), forehead fairly steep and broad; some longer heads in eastern figures.
- Face: a *range* from long oval with narrow jaw (common) to broad-cheeked squarish with wide rounded chin; cheekbones a little prominent, sloping outward; eyes set under a modest brow with a slight upper-lid fold in some (fuller fold more in East Baltic faces).
- Nose: high root; mostly longish and straight, a fair share with a hump or hook (especially older north-western men), a minority short and up-tilted; medium lips, calm.
- Brow ridges and glabella marked on north-western men.

Stature and build (1981 adults): men medium-tall, sturdily built, broad-shouldered, thick-set rather than lanky; women medium height, solid; north-western figures taller and heavier-boned; south-eastern figures a touch slighter.

Everyday look 1975–85 (not folk costume):
- Rural / small-town women, middle-aged and old: cotton headscarf knotted under the chin (patterned or plain), knee-length skirt, dark cloth coat or quilted jacket, cardigan, thick stockings, low sturdy shoes or rubber boots; in rain a clear plastic cape or umbrella (Požerskis 1984).
- Urban women: hair short and set, or permed with volume in the 1980s; younger women with longer straight hair, ponytail or plait; home-sewn or tailor-made dresses and skirt suits, knitted jumpers, restrained colours; widened shoulders and oversize cuts arriving in the mid/late 1980s (Sviderskaitė 2013).
- Men: short hair side-parted or combed back (Sutkus), fuller over the ears in the late 1970s; moustaches common, beards rare (**general knowledge, unverified in text**); flat cloth caps (kepurė su snapeliu) and berets on older and rural men (Sutkus *Teenagers*); fur hats and heavy overcoats in winter; dark jackets, checked shirts, knitted cardigans and V-neck jumpers; workers' quilted jackets (vatinukas). Jeans rare and prized among the young.
- Children: cropped hair with short straight fringes, school uniforms (dark pinafore with white collar for girls; dark suit for boys), Pioneer scarves on occasion (Sutkus 1964/1975).
- Glasses: heavy plastic or thin metal frames, plain (**general knowledge, unverified**).
- Sleeves and trouser legs slightly short on tall men — a documented joke about all-Union size tables (Sviderskaitė 2013).
- Palette: muted — browns, greys, olive, dusty blue, dull red, off-white; occasional stronger colour on a headscarf or knitted jumper.

## 4. Drop-in "people block" (≤70 words, positive phrasing)

> Ordinary Lithuanians of 1981, anonymous types, sturdily built. Hair ash-blond, light-brown or chestnut, straight or faintly wavy, some darker; eyes grey, blue-grey or greenish; fair skin. Broad-cheeked oval faces, high straight or humped noses, rounded chins, calm closed mouths, gaze slightly off the viewer. Men side-parted or cropped, some moustached, in flat caps; women short-set or in cotton headscarves; plain loose clothes in brown, olive, dusty blue, grey, dull red.

(70 words. "Correct natural body proportions" from the current block was dropped only for length; keep it elsewhere in the prompt if the deck relies on it.) Variant tails, swap in by scene: *"older north-western men taller, heavy-browed, hook-nosed"*; *"south-eastern faces a shade darker and slimmer"*; *"children cropped fair hair, short fringes, freckles"*.
