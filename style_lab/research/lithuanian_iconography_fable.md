# Lithuanian iconography for the GO deck — details on top of the 1981 setting
Research note, 2026-10-10. Scope: visual details that read as *Lithuanian* at flashcard scale and sit inside a 1981 Soviet-style civil-defence poster world where Lithuania is free (tricolour only, no heraldry, no Soviet state symbols). Everything cited was read; items marked **unverified** come from general knowledge or a source I could not open.

## (a) Evidence

### A1. Built environment — the traditional farmstead (sodyba)
Main source: VLE, *lietuvių tradicinės sodybos* — https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/lietuviu-tradicines-sodybos/ (long article; read in full). Supporting VLE articles: *troba* https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/troba/ ; *pirkia* https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/pirkia/ ; *stuba* https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/stuba/ ; *klėtis* https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/kletis/ ; *lėkis* https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/lekis/ ; *čiukuras* https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/ciukuras/ ; *šulinys* https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/sulinys/ ; *lietuvių tradicinės gyvenvietės* https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/lietuviu-tradicines-gyvenvietes/ ; *lietuvių liaudies architektūra* https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/lietuviu-liaudies-architektura/ . Confidence: high (encyclopaedic, with bibliography: Butkevičius 1971, Šešelgis 1980–98, Gimbutas 1999/2002, Bertašiūtė 2007).

- **Composition.** An old farmstead had 4–5 buildings: dwelling, granary (svirnas/klėtis), byre (tvartas), threshing barn (kluonas), bathhouse (pirtis); rich farms up to 20, poor ones 1–2. Buildings sit around one, two or three yards (*gerasis*, *ūkinis*, *kluoniena*), with orchard, flower garden (*gėlių darželis*) in front of the house, single trees, and "smulkioji architektūra": crosses, koplytstulpiai, fences, gates, wells, benches, beehives. The street-facing façade "puošė gėlių darželiai, kryžius arba koplytstulpis". The well is dug in the clean yard near the house; ponds behind the byres for watering stock and retting flax. Barns and bathhouses stand apart at the end of the plot against fire.
- **Regional dwellings** (VLE names four types): *troba* (west and south — Žemaitija), *gryčia* (north and central), *pirkia* (south-east — Aukštaitija/Dzūkija), *stuba* (south-west — Suvalkija, Lithuania Minor).
  - *Žemaitija — troba*: long, wide, two-ended log house of hewn logs, sometimes boarded vertically; roof half-hipped or four-slope, 2/3–3/4 of the building's height, "dažniausiai su čiukurais, plačiomis pastogėmis" (gable vents, wide eaves); a massive porch; exterior ornament = lėkiai, fretwork window surrounds, profiled beam-ends; interior with a central chimney-kitchen (*kaminas*) for smoking meat. Žemaitija interiors "vyravo sodrios ryškios spalvos" (rich, strong colours). Villages: clustered (*kupetiniai*) and old single farmsteads with 2–3 yards, ponds, many trees. Wooden koplytstulpiai and Rūpintojėlis figures are most common here.
  - *Aukštaitija — gryčia/pirkia*: simpler 3–6-room single- or two-ended houses; rich NE Aukštaitija farmers built cross-plan "rūmas". Street villages (*gatviniai kaimai*) predominated — houses in a row along one street, "galu į gatvę" (gable to the street) on narrow strips. Interiors "šviesios, naudota daug baltos spalvos" (light, much white).
  - *Dzūkija — pirkia*: round or hewn logs with projecting corner joints ("iškištais galais"); four-slope roof with a *čiukuras*, sometimes slightly curved slopes, wide eaves; gable boards decorated with little windows, weather-vanes and lėkiai; "langinės, tapytos augaliniais raštais arba dažytos 2 spalvomis" (shutters painted with plant patterns or in two colours); porch (*prieangis*) under a small gable roof, from the early 20th c. a glazed porch (*gonkelės*). Small loose forest villages (*bendrės*); small granaries with gable-end doors. Dzūkai and Suvalkians "mėgo margus, raštuotus audinius" (liked colourful patterned textiles).
  - *Suvalkija / Lithuania Minor — stuba*: symmetrical, rectangular, raised eaves with a boarded knee-wall (*ertikis*), gable (rarely hipped) roof of straw, shingle, and "nuo 19 a. vidurio dažnai ir keraminėmis čerpėmis" (red clay tiles from the mid-19th c.); porch open or boarded, later glazed; from c. 1900 increasingly boarded and decorated with fretwork shutters, window surrounds, bargeboards, cornices. Farmsteads around a single rectangular yard, planted round with 1–3 rows of deciduous trees; a pond by the byre.
  - *Curonian Spit / Nemunas delta fishermen*: two-ended houses split by a 2 m corridor; farmsteads oriented to the river/lagoon, smaller outbuildings. (Lithuania Minor also has the most elaborate lėkiai — VLE *lėkis*.)
- **Roof ornaments (lėkiai, also arkliukai, žirgeliai, paukščiukai, ragai, gaidžiai)**: crossed or single boards fixed at the gable ends on the ridge, in the shape of horse heads, horns, birds, plants, people or geometric forms; paired (two symmetrically crossed horse-head/horn boards) is the oldest type; the single fretwork board is used on porches; the most complex in Lithuania Minor and on the coast; believed to protect the house from evil. (VLE *lėkis*.)
- **Granary (klėtis/svirnas)**: on corner stones or oak blocks, 0.5–1 m off the ground; horizontal logs; gable roof of straw or shingle; gable ends "puošiami išdrožtomis dvailų (dvigubų) žirgelių, paukščių, ragų figūromis"; an open front gallery (*prieklėtis*) 1–2 m deep on carved posts (birds, grass-snakes, horses), sometimes joined by flat arches; low doors (~1.4 m) decorated with suns, rosettes, fir-trees, spirals; tiny triangular, square, rhombic, half-round or cross-shaped windows. (VLE *klėtis*.)
- **Fences**: yard, garden and orchard fences are dense wattle (*žiogrių* — woven rods, 1.2–1.6 m) or paling of thin poles/half-logs (1.2–1.3 m); field fences are sparser horizontal-rail types ~1–1.2 m; stone walls ~1 m in stony districts; stiles (*lipynės*) where paths cross. (VLE *sodybos*, "Smulkioji architektūra".)
- **Wells**: shaft wells with a cribbing of split oak logs, thick planks or stone; the sweep well (*šulinys su svirtimi*) spread in the late 19th–early 20th c.; from the early 20th c. concrete rings, lids, and a small two- or four-slope roof over the well, usually with a windlass (*velenas*); pumps in bigger Klaipėda-region and Suvalkija farms. The VLE article carries a 1936 photo "šulinys su svirtimi" (I. Končius). (VLE *šulinys*.)
- **Interiors (traditional)**: table in the brightest corner opposite the door; long benches along the walls; spoon-rack by the stove; beds, dowry chest (*kraičio skrynia*), cradle in the dark corner behind the stove; loom in winter; the "good end" (*seklyčia*) with better oak/ash furniture, towel-rail (*rankšluostinė*), sometimes a mirror or clock. (VLE *sodybos*, *troba*.)
- **Settlement history that matters for 1981**: by 1939 almost all villages had been divided into single farmsteads (*vienkiemiai*); interwar farmsteads were compact, around a square yard, with an almost square house plan. Under Soviet rule "sunaikinta 95 % sodybų, valstiečiai sukelti į naujai suformuotas kolūkių gyvenvietes" (VLE *gyvenvietės*). In the counterfactual free Lithuania, the interwar vienkiemis — square yard, orchard, lėkiai on the gable, well with a roof, wattle fence, cross at the gate — is the correct baseline, not the kolūkis settlement.

### A2. Built environment — Soviet-era and town
- **Soviet-era architecture, VLE *Lietuvos architektūra 1945–1990*** — https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/lietuvos-architektura-1945-1990/ . Mass prefab large-panel housing to standard designs in the 1960s–80s; named districts: Žirmūnai (1962–67), **Lazdynai** (1974; architects V. E. Čekanauskas, V. Brėdikis et al.; Lenin Prize 1974 — VLE *Lazdynai* https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/lazdynai/ : begun 1967, set among pine woods on the Neris), Dainava and Kalniečiai in Kaunas. Rural public buildings of the 1970s–80s (culture houses, kolūkis offices) deliberately used "šlaitiniai stogai" and open timber beams from folk architecture (Mindūnai 1975, Juknaičiai 1982, Punia 1986). Restaurants of the 1970s (Lokys 1972, Medininkai 1975 in Vilnius; Medžiotojų užeiga 1974 in Kaunas) had folk-imitation interiors. Vilnius bus station 1974, LITEXPO 1980, Opera 1974, hotel Lietuva 1983. Confidence: high.
- **Vilnius TV tower** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilnius_TV_Tower : built 1974–1980 (body text; infobox says 1978), 326.5 m, concrete shaft with a reinforced-concrete "saucer" deck at 165 m and a 136 m steel spike; in Karoliniškės. Confidence: moderate (Wikipedia). Visually: a thin shaft with one disc — recognisable at small size.
- **Kaunas interwar modernism** — UNESCO WHL 1661 https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1661/ : "Modernist Kaunas: Architecture of Optimism, 1919–1939", inscribed 2023; ~1,500 interwar buildings; Naujamiestis and the Žaliakalnis garden suburb (1923); named: Christ's Resurrection Church, Research Laboratory, Ąžuolynas sports complex. Confidence: high for the facts; the page gives little visual description (pale render, rounded corners, ribbon windows are **unverified** generalisations from my own knowledge).
- **Vilnius old town**: not researched here; the deck already has its own treatment. Baroque towers, narrow courtyards, red tile roofs — **unverified** in this note.
- **Typical 1960s–80s rural detached house** (silicate-brick, one-and-a-half storeys, asbestos-slate roof): no source read; **unverified**; and in the counterfactual it is the thing to *replace* with the interwar farmhouse.

### A3. Sacred / folk markers
- **Cross-crafting** — UNESCO ICH https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/cross-crafting-and-its-symbolism-00013 (proclaimed 2001, inscribed 2008): oak crosses "about one to five metres high", "many have a small roof and floral or geometric ornamentation", small statues of the Virgin and saints; stand "along roads, at village entrances, beside monuments, and in cemeteries"; under the Soviet regime "the crosses were officially banned". VLE *kryždirbystė* https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/kryzdirbyste/ : in Dzūkija crosses are decorated with wreaths, flowers, sashes and little aprons; in Žemaitija Marian figures are dressed and hung with beads. Confidence: high.
- **Koplytstulpis** (VLE https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/koplytstulpiai/ ): a post 2–3 m high (wooden; brick ones 4–9 m) carrying a small open or closed chapel-box for figures of Christ, Mary or saints; wooden ones mostly in Žemaitija; placed at roadsides, crossroads, farmsteads, cemeteries. **Stogastulpis** (VLE https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/stogastulpiai/ ): a 3–8 m shaft with one to three little roofs, each on 2 or 4 curved brackets, polychrome figures beneath, an openwork iron *saulutė* (sun-cross) on top; edges carved with geometric, animal or plant fretwork. **Koplytėlė** (VLE https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/koplyteles/ ): a miniature church/bell-tower/porch-shaped box, sometimes hung on a tree trunk or a house wall, painted, with a small wooden or iron cross on the roof. Confidence: high.
- **Rūpintojėlis** (VLE https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/rupintojelis/ ): seated Christ, head resting on the right hand, crown of thorns, half-naked with a loincloth (in folk art red or brown, sometimes green); polychrome wood; set in koplytstulpiai, koplytėlės and house interiors, mostly Žemaitija; the term dates from the 1920s–30s. Confidence: high.
- **Užgavėnės** (VLE https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/uzgavenes/ ): masked processions through the village — beggar, "gypsy", travelling Jewish merchant, witch, Death, devil, goat, cock, crane; the straw effigy Morė (Kotrė) in Žemaitija, Gavėnas near Kupiškis; Lašininis vs Kanapinis; pancakes. In the second half of the 20th c. the festival was largely forgotten "išskyrus Žemaitiją". Carved wooden masks as the typical visual form: **unverified** here (general knowledge). Note the ethnic caricature characters — do not draw them.
- **Straw gardens (sodai)** — UNESCO ICH 2023 https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/sodai-straw-garden-making-in-lithuania-01987 : "hanging ornaments made from the stalks of grains" (rye), geometric structures "believed to reflect the pattern of the universe", hung "over the cradles of babies and over a wedding or family table", shown at Easter and Christmas. Confidence: high.
- **Margučiai** — lt.wikipedia *Margutis* (API extract): eggs dyed and patterned by wax-resist or scratching; one of the Easter symbols. Confidence: moderate.
- **Amber** (VLE *gintaras* https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/gintaras/ ): yellow in many shades, rarer white, brown, bluish; collected on the Baltic shore since antiquity; the 1976 National Geographic map's Lithuanian figure wears amber-coloured bead strands (see earlier note). Confidence: high.
- **Sashes (juostos)** and **linen**: no source opened (VLE slug not found); **unverified** in this note. Keep as low-key textile pattern only.
- **Wooden churches** (VLE *lietuvių liaudies medinė sakralinė architektūra* https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/lietuviu-liaudies-medine-sakraline-architektura/ ): ~300 surviving, mostly wooden; "vyrauja bebokštės bažnyčios" (towerless predominate), some with two towers, some with a portico front; Žemaitija churches "monumentalūs, masyvūs, sunkių proporcijų, su aukštais stogais", Aukštaitija lighter with lower roofs; free-standing bell-towers 1–4 stages, lower than the church, the finest 18th-c. pyramidal ones in Žemaitija. Confidence: high.

### A4. Everyday 1975–85 Lithuania-specific objects
- **Šilelis** portable TV — https://dizainaskaune.lt/silelis/ and VLE *televizorių gamyba* https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/televizoriu-gamyba/ : Kaunas Radio Factory; development from 1970, 401D in series from 1972 (VLE says from 1973), black-and-white, 16 cm screen, 3.6 kg, high demand across the USSR, exported to 30+ countries; colour sets from 1978. **Tauras** stationary TVs, Šiauliai (from 1968). **Ekranas**, Panevėžys (VLE https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/ekranas-1/ ): picture-tube factory founded 1962, colour tubes from 1978 — it made tubes, not branded sets. Confidence: high.
- **Vairas bicycles**, Šiauliai (VLE *Baltik vairas* https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/baltik-vairas/ ): factory named Vairas from 1964; models Ereliukas (teenagers, from 1951), Kregždutė (girls), Venta and Rambynas folding bikes; 1986 output ~390,000 Ereliukas. Confidence: high.
- **Vilma** (Vilnius tape-recorder plant), **Elfa**, **Neris cigarettes**, **Spauda kiosks**: not verified (pages not reachable); **unverified**.
- **Cars/buses**: Lithuania built no cars; the Soviet fleet (Žiguli/VAZ, Moskvich, Zaporozhets; Ikarus and LAZ buses) is **unverified** here but uncontroversial. In the counterfactual, any small boxy 1970s saloon reads fine; keep badges blank.
- **Food**: *cepelinai/didžkukuliai* (VLE https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/didzkukuliai/ ): oblong dumplings of grated raw potato with minced meat (or curd, bacon, mushrooms), served with fat, cracklings and sour-cream sauce; spread from East Prussia in the early 20th c. *Šaltibarščiai* (LRT 2020 https://www.lrt.lt/naujienos/laisvalaikis/13/1161403/... ): "burokėliai, agurkas, krapai ir kefyras", a "rausvą sriubą" (pink soup), the modern version "tarpukariu" (interwar), a summer dish. *Kūčiukai/prėskučiai* (VLE https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/preskuciai/ ): small poppy-seed wheat buns baked for Christmas Eve, eaten with poppy milk. Confidence: high. Rye bread, curd, herring, linen tablecloths: **unverified** here but standard.

### A5. Interwar design language
- **Naujoji Romuva** 1931–40, Kaunas (VLE https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/naujoji-romuva/ ): first to use "ryškų, stambų šriftą, naujovišką montažą" (bold large type, modern montage); printed Western art reproductions. **Spaudos fondas** 1921–40 (VLE https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/spaudos-fondas/ ): teachers' cooperative publisher-printer, 857 titles, most school textbooks, *Lietuviškoji enciklopedija* 1931–40, series "Lietuvių klasikai", "Jaunimo skaitymai"; also made exercise books, maps, posters, book covers. **Sakalas** publishing company 1924–40, Kaunas (lt.wikipedia API extract). Periodicals verified via VLE: *Trimitas* (Riflemen's Union weekly, 1920–40, https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/trimitas-1/ ), *Karys* (army weekly, 1919–40, https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/karys-1/ ), *Vairas* (1929–40, https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/vairas-1/ ), *Židinys* (1924–40, https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/zidinys-3/ ), *Lietuvos aidas* (daily 1928–40; and the daily *Lietuva* in Kaunas 1919–28, https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/lietuvos-aidas/ ), *Jaunoji karta* (1928–40, https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/jaunoji-karta/ ), *Ūkininkas* (1918–39, https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/ukininkas-2/ ), *Lietuvos ūkininkas* (1905–40, https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/lietuvos-ukininkas/ ), *Lietuvos žinios* (1909–40, https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/lietuvos-zinios/ ), *Kultūra* (1923–41, lt.wikipedia). Confidence: high for existence and dates; I did not view cover images, so typeface details beyond the Naujoji Romuva sentence are **unverified**.

### A6. Landscape
- **White stork** (VLE *gandriniai* https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/gandriniai/ ): "išrinktas Lietuvos nacionaliniu paukščiu", ~20,000 nests (2022), lives in open country "prie žmonių"; white with black flight feathers, red bill and legs; leaves late August, returns March. Confidence: high. Nests on poles/chimneys/old cartwheels: **unverified** here, standard.
- **Curonian Spit** (VLE https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/kursiu-nerija/ ): 98 km sand peninsula; beach 10–50 m, man-made foredune ridge 4–12 m, then the *palvė* plain, then the great dune ridge; settlements Juodkrantė, Pervalka, Preila, Nida. Confidence: high.
- Lakes (Aukštaitija), pine on sand (Dzūkija), birch, flax fields: not separately sourced; **unverified** here but uncontroversial. Note the Pugainiai/Neris connection is personal context, not evidence.

## (c) Example staging lines (one subtle Lithuanian detail, GO style unchanged)
Format: word — base GO scene → added detail (≤1 clause).
- **namas / house** — plain frontal house, figure at the door → log house with wide eaves, crossed horse-head boards at the ridge, glazed porch with small panes.
- **kaimas / village** — row of houses, road, figures → single farmsteads spaced apart, each with orchard, wattle fence, a roofed sweep well, one tall roadside cross at the junction.
- **knyga / book** — figure reading at a table → hardcover with a plain bold interwar title (see §d), linen tablecloth, small portable TV switched off behind.
- **bažnyčia / church** — building, path, figures → towerless timber church, high roof, separate squat bell-tower, oak cross with little roof by the gate.
- **laukas / field** — flat field, horizon, figure → rye field edged by birch, a stork on a pole, blue flax strip beyond.
- **valgyti / eat** — figure at table with bowl → pink beet-kefir soup, dill, a plate of hot potatoes, rye loaf on linen.
- **miestas / city** — street, blocks, figures → nine-storey panel blocks among pines, thin TV tower with one disc behind.
- **mokykla / school** — children at desks → dark pinafores with white collars, cropped fringes, a straw sodas hung in the window corner.
- **ežeras / lake** — water, shore, figure → long lake under pines, wooden rowing boat drawn up, reed edge.
- **duona / bread** — loaf on table → dark round rye loaf on a linen cloth, knife, clay jug, wooden spoon rack on the log wall.

Rules of thumb that follow from the evidence: use *one* marker per card; prefer architecture and food (read instantly, carry no text) over sacred objects (strong but kitsch-prone and heraldry-adjacent); keep every cross, shrine and book face free of lettering; the interwar vienkiemis, not the kolūkis settlement, is the correct rural baseline for a free 1981 Lithuania.
