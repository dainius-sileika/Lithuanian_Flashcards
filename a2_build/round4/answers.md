## 1. A2-0667 *banko skyrius* — "Is this a historical bank?"

Half of it. The settings row (`a2_build/historical/implementation/settings_rows.csv`, also
`style_lab/research/settings_assignments.csv` row A2-0667, added 2026-10-19 as OPTIONAL, low priority)
is `kaunas_bank_of_lithuania, interior`, and the staging reads: *"a bank branch hall — a row of counters
with cashiers behind glass, a customer counting banknotes at the counter, a heavy round vault door at the
back; through the tall window the pale columned neoclassical front of the central bank across the
street."*

- **The hall the people stand in is generic** — "a branch", invented. No real Lithuanian branch office
  was identified for it.
- **The building seen through the window is historical:** the Bank of Lithuania head office in Kaunas,
  corner of K. Donelaičio and Maironio streets, architect Mykolas Songaila, cornerstone 12 March 1925,
  opened 8 December 1928, neoclassical; two-storey Operations Hall with a Corinthian colonnade under a
  large triangular skylight, black-marble Ionic columns in the lobby, Swedish granite stair, a 3-tonne
  vault door (Bank of Lithuania Money Museum guide,
  https://www.pinigumuziejus.lt/en/eguide/the-bank-of-lithuanias-building-in-kaunas ; the bank entry
  also cites https://autc.lt/en/architecture-object/?id=661 — not re-read this pass).
- The same building's **hall** is the main picture of **055 bankas** ("Good location" — owner), so the
  two bank cards are tied to one real place: 055 inside it, 0667 looking at it from across the street.

If the owner wants the branch itself historical, the honest answer is that the deck has no verified
interwar branch interior; the Bank's own Operations Hall is already spent on 055. Leave 0667 as it is.

## 2. A2-0649 *paštas* — "a prettier historical post office … or don't use the outside insert"

**Current place:** Kaunas Central Post Office, Laisvės al. 102, by Feliksas Vizbaras, built 1931 (the bank entry says 1930–32);
UNESCO-listed with Kaunas modernism in 2023 (`location_bank.json` `kaunas_central_post_office`, sources
https://kaunas2022.eu/modernizmasateiciai/en/kauno-centrinis-pastas/index.html ,
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaunas_Central_Post_Office). The owner's eye is right: the **exterior is a
smooth, flat-roofed modernist block** whose merit is proportion and the carved window frames — it does
not carry at inset scale. **The interior is the famous part:** the tall hall with the floor tiled in a
Lithuanian woven-linen (*rankšluostis*) pattern, brass-railed counters, an electric clock on the hall's front wall; in 1931 the flat roof and big
windows "weren't of everyone's liking" while the floor was praised (kaunas2022.eu page above). That interior is already the card's main picture.

**Alternatives looked at**

| Building | Why / why not |
|---|---|
| **Klaipėda Post Office, Liepų g. 16** — red-brick neo-Gothic "post palace", architect H. Schoede, opened 16 Oct 1893; two-storey centre with attics and gables, two wings (stables and carriage house), a tower; the hall interior survives; still a working post office (https://krastogidas.lt/en/objects/klaipeda-post-office) | The one genuinely pretty historical post-office **exterior** in Lithuania. But: a Prussian building (Memel was German until 1923; fine in 1981 Klaipėda), and the tower's carillon dates from 1987 — draw the tower without bells. Using it as the *inset* of a card whose *main picture* is the Kaunas hall would make the card's two "post offices" different buildings; using it as the whole card loses the Kaunas floor. |
| Vilnius Central Post Office, Gedimino pr. 7 | Not verified this pass (building history unclear); not proposed. |
| Keep Kaunas, drop the outside | Zero risk; keeps the real place and the floor the owner likes. |

**Recommendation: interior main scene, no exterior inset.** Replace inset 1 with the pre-roll-out inset
("a plain blue post box by the door, letters going into the slot") by masked edit — `plan.csv` row
A2-0649, marked *owner to confirm*. If the owner wants a handsome post-office *front* somewhere in the
deck, the place for Klaipėda is a **different** card (e.g. A2-1326 *paštininkas* if it is ever re-rolled
again, or a *laiškas*/*siųsti* card), not this one. Not ordered.

## 3. 122 *menininkas* (Čiurlionis) — does the setting suit him?

**Current staging** (`card_notes.json`): *"A man and a woman artist painting at easels outdoors above a
river, crooked houses on the slope; the man slim, about thirty, dark hair swept back and a dark moustache,
painting a canvas of two crowned figures bending over a small glowing globe in a dark forest
[Karalių pasaka]…"* **Setting** (`settings_assignments.csv` row 122): `vilnius_uzupis, exterior` —
"small fast river under a stone bridge, crooked old houses climbing a slope, church towers across the
water", i.e. the Vilnia at Užupis, Vilnius.

**Does Vilnius suit him?** Yes. Čiurlionis came to Vilnius on 31 December 1906 for the First Lithuanian
Art Exhibition (Vileišis palace, Antakalnis) and **lived in the city in 1907–1908**, renting a room on
Šv. Andriejaus (now Savičiaus) street in the Old Town — a few hundred metres from the Vilnia and Užupis —
while he ran the Lithuanian Art Society's affairs and painted
(https://neakivaizdinisvilnius.lt/en/studies/following-in-the-footsteps-of-m-k-ciurlionis ; the VLE
article https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/mikalojus-konstantinas-ciurlionis/ could not be fetched this pass,
so the dates above rest on the Neakivaizdinis Vilnius page). *Karalių pasaka* (1909) belongs to exactly
this Vilnius–St Petersburg period.

**The one false note, stated plainly:** Čiurlionis did not paint *Karalių pasaka* at an easel on a
riverbank; his pictures are tempera works from imagination, made indoors. The card's plein-air easel is a
conceit the *artist* card needs (two artists at work, outdoors, legible at a glance), not biography. It
is a wink, not an error, and the owner has already called the result "very good".

**Counter-hypothesis tested:** Druskininkai would be the *more* Čiurlionis place (family home, now his
memorial museum; the *Raigardas* triptych of 1907 is one of his few works tied to a real landscape, the
Raigardas valley by Druskininkai). It would be the better setting **only for a fresh re-roll** — and a
re-roll puts the face and the painting-on-the-canvas, both of which landed, back at risk.

**Decision: the setting suits — leave the card.** No plan row.

## 4. Direction of movement — should "main actors head right" be a standing rule?

**Recommendation: yes, as a default with named exceptions, applied at re-roll only** (no retroactive
re-rolls). The deck already leans this way in three places: `MOTION_STRIP_RULE` reads its three phases
"left to right"; the *vėjas* staging (deck_builder row 303) bends everything "to the RIGHT"; the
two-panel situation grammar puts the ask on the LEFT and the answer on the RIGHT. A standing rule makes
the habit explicit and stops the engine from choosing at random.

**Why it is a default, not an absolute.** Three classes of card must be free to point the other way:
(a) cards whose *meaning is a direction or a side* — on the right / on the left, turn left, back,
return/come home (grįžti, where coming toward the viewer or leftward reads as "back"); (b) two-figure
conversation scenes where the sides are already fixed by who speaks first; (c) scenes where the goal
object is placed on the left for a reason (a door, a counter) — there the figure walks toward the goal,
and the rule yields.

**Draft rule text** (for `go_generator.py`, owner's wording to approve; the executor adds it to the
standing rules for noun/verb scene cards and to the situation lead, after `ARROW_RULE`):

> `DIRECTION_RULE = 'When the main figures walk, run, ride, drive or carry something from one place to another, they travel from the viewer's LEFT toward the RIGHT, the way a page is read; the place they are going to lies to the right of them. Figures who stand, sit or talk are not affected.'`

**Routing:** on by default for every card that gets `MOTION_STRIP_RULE` or whose staging contains a verb
of motion; off (not emitted) for rows in a new `deck_builder.DIRECTION_FREE_ROWS` set — the side/direction
cards (A2-2005 *dešinėje*, its *kairėje* pair, *tiesiai*/*atgal* cards, *grįžti*), the two-panel
`situation_2` rows, and any row whose staging names a side with LEFT/RIGHT in capitals (the staging
wins). QA: add one line to `FIT_PROMPT` — "do the main moving figures head right?" — as a WARN, not a
FAIL.

**Cost:** a prompt sentence; no renders until a card is re-rolled anyway. **Risk:** the engine may mirror
a scene to comply and hand a prop to the wrong hand; stagings that name LEFT/RIGHT hands already cover
this, and the rule says nothing about hands.

The owner decides; the A2-0660 re-roll in `plan.csv` carries the instruction regardless.
