## The top proposals

Places (each with a description the picture prompt can use, and a source):
- **Court, judge, lawyers, Seimas** → the Palace of Justice and Seimas in Kaunas (1925–30): the arcaded neoclassical corner block that housed the Supreme Tribunal and, from 1936, the parliament. Today the Kaunas Philharmonic.
- **Post office** → Kaunas Central Post Office (1931): flat-roofed modernist block with carved window frames and a hall floor patterned like a woven linen towel.
- **Bank** → Bank of Lithuania, Kaunas (1928): marble lobby, black Ionic columns, glass skylight over the hall.
- **Museum** → the War Museum with the Čiurlionis gallery (1936), its bell tower and garden; the orange Lituanica hangs inside.
- **Gym and basketball** → Kaunas Sports Hall (1939), four great steel arches; Europe's first basketball hall and the place Lithuania won the 1939 title.
- **Stadium** → the State Stadium in Kaunas (1925), which Steponas Darius himself designed and built.
- **Town square** → Kaunas Town Hall square with the "White Swan" tower.
- **Concert hall, concert, orchestra** → the Vilnius Philharmonic (1902).
- **Café** → Konrado kavinė on Laisvės alėja, the interwar writers' café.
- Smaller: ticket office in Kaunas station; Romuva cinema (1940) as an inset; Rasos cemetery.

Three existing cards are flagged, not overridden: the hospital (the current picture uses a 1970s Soviet block; the 1939 Kaunas Clinics would be truer), the monument (the current "seated poet" is the 1977 Soviet-era Maironis statue; the 1928 Freedom Statue is proposed), and the Seimas card, whose staging asks for the Vytis, which the locked flag-only rule forbids.

## Vetting verdicts
Every person was checked in the Lithuanian encyclopaedia (vle.lt) and, where relevant, against the Genocide and Resistance Research Centre's published positions. Sources are in historical_likenesses.md.

- **Clear:** Darius, Girėnas, Gustaitis, Maironis, Žemaitė, Vaižgantas, Kudirka, Binkis, Sruoga, Basanavičius, Čiurlionis, Zikaras, Lubinas, Jablonskis, Stulginskis, Grinius, Žemaitis. All died before 1940, or were Soviet victims, or emigrated.
- **Caution:** Smetona (came to power by the 1926 coup, dissolved the Seimas in breach of the constitution, banned opposition parties in 1936; never served the Soviets or Nazis). Adamkus (never Soviet; living; served at 18 in a 1944 Lithuanian unit raised under German command to fight the Red Army, a point Russian officials have used against him).
- **Exclude:** Salomėja Nėris (went to Moscow in 1940 with the delegation asking for annexation, wrote the Poem about Stalin, USSR deputy 1941–45); Vincas Krėvė (deputy prime minister in the 1940 puppet government, though he tried to resign); Kipras Petrauskas (Stalin-era state prize 1951); Gediminas Jokūbonis (Lenin Prize 1963, sculptor of the current "monument" picture).

## The president card
You asked for Valdas Adamkus. He is clear of the Soviet machine and a patriot, but: in a free Lithuania of 1981 he would be a 54-year-old American official in Chicago, not president; he is alive (99, in hospital this month), and a public deck drawing a living man without consent is a personal-rights question; and the picture model may refuse or soften a named living politician.

Options: (a) Adamkus as asked; (b) an interwar president — Grinius (cleanest record, a democrat, recognisable beard and glasses), Stulginskis (clean, deported to Siberia in 1941), Smetona (most recognisable, authoritarian caution); (c) an unnamed statesman.

**My recommendation: (c), with interwar dress and the Kaunas Seimas building behind.** If you want a face, Grinius. Adamkus only if you accept the anachronism and the living-person point, and only if the test shows the model will draw him. The decision is yours.

## Feasibility
Faces will not carry at card scale in this style: heads are a few dozen pixels, the style deliberately flattens individuality, and the deck's own locked rule says "figures are types, not individuals". What does carry is the person's world: the plane, the headscarf, the hall, the statue, the building. The deck already does this (interwar army uniform, litas notes, the cathedral statues). So the plan evokes people through things and buildings, keeps every figure anonymous, and adds one small prompt addition for approved cards only; the existing style rules stay untouched. Whether the model refuses a named living person is unknown until tested.
