# The president card (097 prezidentas / prezidentė)

Plan only (2026-10-17).

Current staging: a man president at a tricolour-draped podium speaking, a woman president at a side table signing; insets of a handshake, a flag before a government building, a motorcade. Dual-gender card by locked decision (Q-dual, 19 person nouns).

The owner asked for Valdas Adamkus. The honest assessment follows, then three options and a recommendation. The choice is the owner's.

## 1. Adamkus, vetted

Facts (Visuotinė lietuvių enciklopedija, https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/valdas-adamkus/ unless stated):
- Born 3 November 1926, Kaunas. As an Aušra gymnasium pupil he took part in the anti-Nazi resistance: member of the Lithuanian Freedom Fighters' Union, co-publisher and editor of the underground paper Jaunime, budėk!.
- "1944 su tėvais pasitraukė į Vokietiją" (left for Germany with his parents in 1944). Eichstätt Lithuanian gymnasium 1946; Munich; settled in the USA 1949, citizen 1955; civil engineer (IIT 1960).
- US environmental administrator: deputy head of the fifth (largest) region 1970–81, head 1981–97; led US–USSR environmental cooperation.
- Émigré leader: Santara-Šviesa, Margutis radio for about 25 years, World Lithuanian Games 1983 and 1991; backed Sąjūdis from 1988; one of the initiators of the restored Vytautas Magnus University.
- President 1998–2003 and 2004–09 (gave up US citizenship).
- Living: in Santaros clinics for further treatment as of 9 October 2026, turning 100 on 3 November 2026 (LRT, https://www.lrt.lt/naujienos/lietuvoje/2/3077190/valdas-adamkus-lieka-ligonineje-tolesniam-gydymui).

Soviet machine: nothing. He never lived under Soviet rule as an adult and his whole émigré career was anti-Soviet. **Clear.**

Nazi-era question: by his own spokesman's account (JTA, 12 February 1999, https://jta.org/?p=400954, answering an allegation by a Russian Duma committee chairman that he "fought on the side of the Nazis"), after returning briefly from Germany in 1944 at 18 he joined the "Homeland Defense Force". That is the Tėvynės apsaugos rinktinė, formed 29 July 1944 "prie H. Mäderio kovos grupės" (attached to German Kampfgruppe Mäder) from mostly 18–20-year-old Lithuanians to hold the front against the Red Army near Seda; it was broken on 7 October 1944 (https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/sedos-kautynes/). Lithuanian historiography treats it as a Lithuanian anti-Soviet unit raised under German authority, not an SS or police formation; VLE's Adamkus article does not mention it. For this deck: not a collaboration finding, but it is the one thing hostile parties have used against him, and a public deck should know that. **Caution, not exclude.**

The real problem is different: in a free Lithuania of 1981 Adamkus is a 54-year-old American civil servant in Chicago. There is no path by which he is president in that year, so his likeness is a wink at the owner's own timeline, not at the deck's. And he is alive. Drawing a living person into a public deck, even flatteringly, is a personality-rights question (Lithuanian Civil Code art. 2.22 is titled "Teisė į atvaizdą", the right to one's image — confirmed only from the table of contents at https://www.e-tar.lt/portal/lt/legalAct/TAR.8A39C83848CB/asr ; I could not read the article body in this pass, so I make no claim about the scope of its public-figure exception; the owner should read it before relying on it). A deck card is in any case not reporting on his public activity.

## 2. The three options

### (a) Adamkus, as asked
- For: the owner's wish; the one president everyone agrees was never part of the Soviet system; recognisable to Lithuanians (tall, lean, white hair swept back, long face, glasses in later years).
- Against: anachronistic for 1981; living person in a public deck; the model may soften or refuse a named living politician (feasibility.md); the 1944 episode is a stick others have used.

### (b) An interwar president
All three are dead, pre-Soviet, and plausible as "the president" in a card that is not dated to the year.

| President | Term | Vetting | Visual handle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Antanas Smetona | 1919–20; 1926–40 | Clear of Soviet or Nazi service: proposed armed resistance to the Soviet ultimatum on 15 June 1940, then left for Germany and Switzerland; died in a fire in Cleveland 9 January 1944. **Caution:** came to power by the 17 December 1926 coup; "Su kitais pažeisdamas konstituciją 1927 04 paleido Seimą" (dissolved the Seimas in breach of the constitution); "įvedė autoritarinį prezidentinį režimą"; "1936 uždraudė opozicinių partijų veiklą". https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/antanas-smetona/ | Round pince-nez, small moustache, bald crown, dark suit — the most recognisable interwar face. |
| Aleksandras Stulginskis | acting 1920–22; 1922–26 | **Clear, Soviet victim.** Christian Democrat co-founder; farmed from 1927; "1941 06 SSRS okupacinės valdžios suimtas, be teismo kalintas Krasnojarsko krašto lageriuose ir kalėjimuose"; sentenced to 25 years in 1952; released 1954, exiled to Komi, home 1956; worked at a horticultural research station 1957–60; died Kaunas 1969. https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/aleksandras-stulginskis/ | Clean-shaven, dark hair parted, stern; less recognisable. |
| Kazys Grinius | June–Dec 1926 | **Clear.** Physician; elected 7 June 1926, removed by the coup; signed the 1942 memorandum to the German Commissioner General with Krupavičius and Aleksa protesting colonisation and the mass resettlement of citizens including Jews, Russians and Poles; exiled by the Germans to his home village; left 1944; died Chicago 1950. https://www.vle.lt/straipsnis/kazys-grinius/ | White beard and moustache, round glasses, elderly even in 1926. |

Within (b): Grinius is the cleanest record and a democrat; Stulginskis the cleanest record and the most tragic; Smetona the most recognisable but carries the authoritarian caution, and he is the figure Soviet propaganda spent fifty years painting as a "fascist", which a 1981-set deck might not want to re-open. Note also that Smetona's face is already on the deck in effect: Lietuvos aidas (card 152) was his regime's official paper.

### (c) An unnamed statesman
- A grey-haired man in a dark three-piece suit at a tricolour-draped podium, and a woman president signing, exactly as staged now. The card already works; the English caption now carries the meaning.
- This is also what the locked style rule ("types, not individuals") asks for, and what the feasibility note predicts the model will produce anyway at card scale.

## 3. Recommendation

(c), with a light (b) flavour: keep the figures anonymous, but dress the man president in interwar statesman's style — dark three-piece suit, high stiff collar, a small tricolour ribbon on the lapel, round spectacles — and set the podium in front of the Kaunas Palace of Justice and Seimas (real_places.md). That reads as "president of a Lithuanian republic" without claiming any one man, honours the interwar state without re-litigating 1926, keeps a living man out of a public deck, and does not break the style lock.

If the owner wants a named face anyway: Grinius (option b), because he is clear on every axis and his beard-and-spectacles survive small drawing. Adamkus only if the owner accepts the anachronism and the living-person point and the test render shows the model will draw him; in that case do not name him in any published text.
