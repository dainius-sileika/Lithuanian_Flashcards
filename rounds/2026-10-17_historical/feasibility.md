# Feasibility: likenesses and real places with gpt-image-2.5-sunburst

Plan only (2026-10-17). No renders were made.

## 1. Will a likeness come through?

Verdict: **faces, no; worlds, yes.**

Why faces will not carry:
- Scale. Cards are 864x1152 (3:4) with a main picture plus 2–3 insets; a standing figure's head is roughly 40–80 px high. The production lead asks for "broad-cheeked oval faces, straight noses, calm closed mouths", flat tone on skin and brush-ink outlines. A face drawn to that spec, at that size, cannot carry the difference between Smetona and any other bald man in pince-nez. The style is designed to erase individuality (GO_STYLE_SPEC.md: "Figures are types, not individuals").
- Prompt order. The lead's "anonymous types" sentence comes first and is long; a later "in the likeness of X" clause fights it. In this pipeline the later clause usually loses or produces a generic face with one borrowed attribute.
- Even when a model knows a face (it almost certainly has Smetona, Darius and Adamkus photographs in training; Binkis, Stulginskis or Vaižgantas much less), the gouache-and-halftone finish strips the cues a viewer would use.

Why worlds will carry:
- The deck already proves it: the interwar army uniform (cards 120, 121, 147), the litas banknotes (133, 135), the Lietuvos aidas masthead (152), the cathedral statues (117, A2-1280) all read as "this is a free Lithuania" without a single name in the prompt.
- Objects and buildings are drawn large and have strong silhouettes: an orange high-wing monoplane with aerofoil struts, a hall with four steel arches, a tiered white tower, a headscarf and black dress at a writing table. These survive the style and the scale.

## 2. Will the model refuse real people?

- Reported OpenAI policy since 25 March 2025 (GPT-4o image generation, as reported by CBC News 13 April 2025, https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.7507039): public figures are generated by default, with an opt-out for the person, extra measures for political candidates, and a ban on manipulating images of real people without consent. I could not read OpenAI's current policy text for gpt-image-2.5 in this pass; treat the behaviour as **unverified until tested**.
- Practical expectation: a named dead historical figure (Darius, Maironis) will most likely be accepted; a named living former head of state (Adamkus) may be accepted, softened to a generic face, or refused as a content-policy error. go_generator.py's OpenAI path fails fast on content-policy errors (1.6 hardening), so a refusal costs one call and a log line, not a stuck run.
- Separate from refusal: the generator records no provenance of who is depicted. If a named likeness is used, the card ledger (cards.csv notes) should say so, so a later contributor knows the card carries a real person.

## 3. Reconciling with the prompt rules

The brief calls it "PLAUSIBILITY_RULE's ban on named props". For the record, PLAUSIBILITY_RULE in go_generator.py (line 113) is about objects being complete and in working order; it bans nothing by name. The actual "no names" discipline is spread over three places:
1. the leads' "anonymous types" sentence;
2. the location bank's design: `prompt_phrase` describes a place and `name` is appended only where `naming_the_place_helps` is true (go_settings._line) — i.e. names are already allowed as a tail, never as the carrier;
3. FLAG_RULE: no coats of arms, no Vytis, no Columns of Gediminas (heraldry garbles like text), and no text anywhere (NO_TEXT_RULE), which is why "LITUANICA" on the fuselage or a masthead name cannot be the identifier.

Proposed narrow exception, to be added as one constant and used only by cards the owner approves (the executor decides where it goes in `_prompt_openai`; a new optional `precedent=""` segment inserted after `setting` and before MAIN_MEANING_RULE would fit the F6.1 assembly order):

> PRECEDENT_RULE: "This card quietly evokes a real Lithuanian precedent through its things, not through a face: {description}. Describe-by-features only; the people remain anonymous types of 1981 and no name, emblem or lettering identifies the precedent."

with the per-card `{description}` written in the staging line (e.g. "an orange single-engine high-wing monoplane with wide aerofoil-shaped wing struts and a ring-cowled radial engine"). Rules kept intact: FLAG_RULE, NO_TEXT_RULE, the anonymous-types lead, SETTING_GUARD. Rules touched: none; this is an additive segment, not an edit to existing constants, so the 520 shipped masters still render byte-identically.

If the owner chooses a named face for one card (route 1, e.g. the president), that is a second, separate exception: a `likeness=""` segment reading "The {role} is drawn in the likeness of {name} as photographed around {year}: {three features}; the face is still printed flat in the album style." Use it on at most a handful of cards and record it in the ledger.

## 5. Rights and personality issues for a public deck

- Living people: Adamkus (99). Drawing him into a public, freely distributed deck without consent is the one real exposure in this plan. Recommendation: do not, or ask him (his office exists: https://www.lrvalstybe.lt/valstybine-institucija/kadencija-baiges-lietuvos-respublikos-prezidentas-valdas-adamkus-10849). Civil Code art. 2.22 "Teisė į atvaizdą" governs; its body was not read in this pass.
- Dead people: no personality right survives in a way that touches a drawing, but descendants and foundations can object publicly; Nėris's family went to court over her monument in 2026 (LRT). The vetted list avoids contested figures.
- Buildings: no rights issue in drawing a building's exterior or interior in an illustration.
- Trade marks and lettering: none drawn (no-text rule). The Lituanica's sponsor scrolls and name are dropped.
- Reputational: the counterfactual "free Lithuania 1981" is a fiction; placing a real person's face in it attributes to them a life they did not have. That is harmless for Darius (dead 1933) and awkward for a living man. One more reason for route 2.
