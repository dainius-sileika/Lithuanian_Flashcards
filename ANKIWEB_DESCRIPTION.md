# Lietuvių Flashcards — Lithuanian vocabulary, illustrated

*(Paste the section below into the AnkiWeb description box.)*

---

**520 core Lithuanian words, taught by picture.**

Learn the way *Fluent Forever* recommends — from **images, not translations** — so
each word attaches straight to a concept instead of an English crutch.

Every card is a single, guessable illustration in one consistent house style: the
deadpan, muted look of a **1980s Soviet civil-defence training manual**. Calm
instructional wall-charts, anonymous everyperson figures, numbered insets, the
occasional small tricolour. Almost no text is baked into the art — the picture
does the teaching.

**What's on a card**

- **Front:** the illustration alone. Try to name it before you flip.
- **Back:** the word, its **stress-accented form** (*šuõ*, *nãmas*, *dangùs*),
  **audio**, a gender chip, the grammar forms, and the English gloss.
- **Grammar forms** are chosen to be the ones you actually need: nouns show the
  genitive (which reveals the declension), verbs show the principal parts
  (*šokti · šoka · šoko*), adjectives show the feminine.

**Design rules that make it work**

- **Guessability gate** — if you didn't already know the word, you should be able
  to infer it from the main image alone. Insets support that meaning and never
  vote for a neighbouring word.
- **Opposites share a picture** — every adjective pair (clean/dirty, deep/shallow,
  dark/light…) is drawn as the *same* scene, with only the marking arrow moving to
  the other pole, so you learn the quality rather than the object.

**Please read before you start**

- This is a **community beta**. The illustrations are AI-generated in the manual
  style and have been through five rounds of hand QA, but some cards are better
  than others — corrections are very welcome.
- The **audio is synthesized** (Azure neural voice). It is clear and consistent,
  but its word stress is not always right. Recordings by a native speaker are
  planned to replace it; treat the written accent marks as the authority.
- **Stress accents** are sourced, never invented — from Wiktionary and
  kirtis.info, and each one is accepted only if stripping its marks exactly
  reproduces the plain headword. Coverage is 520/520.
- Glosses and verb principal parts have been checked by a native speaker;
  rule-derived noun genitives and adjective feminines are still being spot-checked.
